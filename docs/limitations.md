# What the map can and cannot say

The map describes relationships in the text you supply. It does not decide factual truth, independence, credibility, plagiarism, or original authorship.

## Exact citations

A URL string can be quoted, criticized, recommended, or listed in a menu. Its presence does not prove that the source supports an associated claim. The initial parser does not infer claim-to-citation entailment. Citation matching does not follow redirects or combine aliases; collect or normalize sources carefully.

## Shared wording

Wording overlap is set containment over normalized five-word phrases. Common navigation, legal notices, or long quotations can dominate a score. Short excerpts can score highly against longer texts. Removing boilerplate and comparing the relevant passage makes the result more useful.

Normalization ignores case and punctuation. It does not stem words, translate languages, identify paraphrases, understand meaning, or know whether a passage was generated by AI. The score is not calibrated as a probability. One shared phrase is shown as a witness, not a complete overlap explanation.

Groups use transitive connectivity. Two sources in the same family need not overlap directly. A missing upstream original can make every candidate misleading. Separate wording families do not prove independent reporting or independent evidence.

## Dates and scale

Dates come from your corpus. They may be wrong, refer to updates, or omit earlier publication. The earliest known date is only a candidate; undated sources and ties remain unresolved. The UI supports 2–20 records and at most 100,000 characters per source. Large inputs can pause the main thread while comparisons run.

## Persistence and third-party content

The browser stores nothing automatically. Export to retain your work. Reports include source text and can carry private or copyrighted material. The optional collector does not bypass access controls, execute JavaScript, or establish redistribution rights. Review collected text before drawing conclusions.
